The Hosting a Large Dinner Guide

Cooking for twelve, or for a hundred, is less about recipes and more about planning. Here is how to keep a big dinner calm.

Plan backwards from the date

  • Four weeks: fix the guest list, venue and menu style.
  • Two weeks: confirm numbers and dietary needs; order anything special.
  • One week: plan seating, borrow or hire tables, chairs and linen.
  • Two days: shop and prepare anything that keeps.
  • On the day: set the table early, cook, then dress for dinner.

Choose a menu that scales

Large dinners are easier with dishes that can be made ahead or cooked in big batches: braises, roasts, baked pasta, grain salads and set desserts. Save last-minute dishes for small groups.

Quantities for a crowd

ItemPer guest
Main protein (boneless)150–180 g
Starchy side150–200 g
Vegetables100–150 g in total
Bread1–2 rolls
Dessert1 portion, plus 10% extra

For buffets, allow a little more: guests tend to try everything.

Service: plated, family-style or buffet

Plated service is elegant but needs help in the kitchen. Family-style platters at each table are warm and efficient. Buffets suit large, informal groups but need space for guests to queue.

Timing the courses

Allow 20–30 minutes for lighter courses and about 45 for the main, with short breaks between. Clear one course fully before bringing the next. The Course Timing Planner sets out a schedule for you.

Ask for help

Assign simple jobs to willing guests or hire help for serving and clearing at larger events. A host who can sit down for the main course makes the evening feel relaxed for everyone.
