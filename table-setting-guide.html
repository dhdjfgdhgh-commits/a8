The Table Setting Guide

A well-set table tells guests the evening has been planned with care. Here is how to set one for any occasion.

Three levels of formality

SettingPiecesUse for
InformalPlate, fork, knife, water glass, napkinFamily dinners, casual gatherings
Semi-formalAdds a salad fork, soup spoon and dessert spoonCelebrations, dinner parties
FormalAdds fish cutlery, bread plate, several glassesBanquets, weddings, milestone dinners

Where everything goes

  • Forks on the left of the plate, in order of use from the outside in.
  • Knives on the right, blades facing the plate; spoons to the right of the knives.
  • Dessert cutlery above the plate, horizontal.
  • Bread plate top left, with a small butter knife across it.
  • Glasses top right, above the knives, water glass nearest.

Napkins

Place the napkin on the plate or to the left of the forks. A simple fold looks more elegant than an elaborate one. Cloth napkins add warmth; choose one colour for the whole table.

Centrepieces and candles

Keep centrepieces low enough that guests can see each other across the table, roughly below chin height when seated. Unscented candles are best near food. For long tables, repeat small arrangements every metre or so rather than one large piece.

Spacing and linen

Allow about 60 cm per guest. A tablecloth should hang 20–30 cm over the edge for a dinner, longer for formal events. Iron or steam linen on the table to remove fold lines.

Build your own setting

Not sure what to put out? The Place Setting Builder lists the exact pieces for the courses you are serving.
